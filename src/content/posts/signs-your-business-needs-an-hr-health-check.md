Most small businesses start with informal HR: a few templates, a handshake approach to policies and a founder who handles every people question personally. That works well at five employees. At twenty or fifty, the cracks begin to show.

An HR health check is a structured look at your people practices. It helps you find gaps before they turn into costly problems. Here are five signs it is time for one.

## 1. Your policies live in people's heads

If the answer to "what is our policy on…?" is "ask the owner", you are relying on memory rather than clear, written guidance. Inconsistent answers lead to frustration and, sometimes, to disputes.

**What to do:** document your core policies — attendance, leave, conduct and workplace harassment — in plain language, and make sure everyone can find them.

## 2. Job descriptions are outdated or missing

Roles change as a business grows. Outdated job descriptions make it harder to hire, set expectations and manage performance fairly.

**What to do:** review each role's purpose, key responsibilities and required skills at least once a year.

## 3. Hiring feels different every time

When each manager hires in their own way, you risk inconsistent decisions and missed talent. A structured process with the same questions and scoring criteria leads to better, fairer hires.

## 4. Performance conversations keep getting postponed

If feedback only happens when something goes wrong, small issues grow. Regular, documented conversations help employees improve and protect the business if problems persist.

## 5. You are not sure you are meeting your obligations

Employment standards, workplace harassment requirements and record-keeping rules change over time. If you are unsure whether your practices are current, that uncertainty is itself a risk.
